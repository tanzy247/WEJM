Bigodi Forest

James our local guide took us to visit the community run wetland sanctuary famous for primate tracking and birdwatching. We were lucky to spot: red-tailed monkeys, black-and-white colobus, vervet monkeys and Uganda red colobus as well as several birds.

Kibale National Park

Chimpanzee Trekking

Whilst in Kibale, we visited the National Park where we got to experience the incredible chimpanzee trekking. After only a short trek in the park, we discovered a family of chimps and were able to spend time watching them interact. It was impressive to see them move through the trees and we couldn’t believe how close we managed to get when they climbed down to the ground.

Bwindi National Park

Our final stop in Uganda was Bwindi National Park. When we first arrived we were given a tour of a local village where we got to visit a community run school.
I thoroughly enjoyed visiting the school and learning more about how the local people live and support one another.

We also had the opportunity to meet a group of Batwa people.

The Batwa people are an indigenous hunter-gatherers who previously lived in Bwindi Forest living off the land before they were displaced from ancestral lands in the early 1990s when national parks were established to protect mountain gorillas.

We were warmly welcomed and taught some of their traditional ways including how to start a fire.

Finally - the highlight of our trip and one of the most incredible experiences we have been fortunate to have was gorilla trekking where we were able to trek to find and observe endangered mountain gorillas in the wild. This was truly phenomenal. We were lucky to find a gorilla family of 14 including 1 silverback as well as 3 babies and 3 juveniles who were extremely playful.

We watched the gorillas munching in the fig trees for almost an hour and then we were lucky enough to watch them come down 1 by 1 where we managed to get a closer look- absolutely incredible!
